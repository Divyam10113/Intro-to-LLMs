Task 3 · Research problem discovery and ideation · base paper MVFA (CVPR 2024)

From thirty-nine limitations of MVFA to ten ranked, codable research ideas

This page follows every step of the pipeline that turned our Task 2 limitations into two research problems (ResearchAgent), grounded seed ideas in citation chains (HypER), evolved them with a genetic algorithm (HypoEvolve) and ranked the results on five criteria. Click any idea to read it in full with its reviews, novelty evidence and family tree.

All LLM calls were answered through the pipeline's file backend by the configured model (claude-opus-5-5) acting as generator and judge; every prompt and answer is stored in logs/cache. This is run 3, built on Task 1's run-v6 output (594-paper corpus with OpenAlex keywords and topics, 59-paper review set, 194 papers citing MVFA together with Semantic Scholar) and Task 2's 39 structured limitations. Following the team's plan, problems were generated only for the two directions Task 4 builds on: label supervision (group A) and scoring (group B). Runs 1 and 2, on earlier versions of the Task 1 review, are archived with the code.

Stage 0 and 0b

Limitations, groups, and what the released code actually does

Task 2's 39 limitations were grouped into seven groups; twelve fit no group. Checking them against MVFA's public code (commit a30bf7b) gave ten limitations a verdict (four confirmed, three open questions resolved, two worse than stated, one corrected) and added four code-only limitations (C1–C4). Groups A and B are the problem sources, and the leakage-free protocol group became the evaluation protocol of every idea.

Three generations of idea evolution

Stage 4 · Multi-criteria ranking

Shortlist, scores and the selected top five

The twelve highest-fitness candidates per problem were reviewed on Novelty, Feasibility, Clarity, Impact and Validity with a literature novelty search. Final score = 0.7 × normalised review mean + 0.3 × normalised fitness; at most two ideas per method family enter the top five.
