## Research problem

The research problem is to develop an annotation-aware, coarse-to-fine extension of MVFA’s multi-level CLIP adapters for transferring small-lesion localization with limited dense supervision. A frozen pretrained CLIP encoder and native-resolution images are available; source adaptation uses normal/abnormal image labels and optional masks, while a source-unseen target supplies either no adaptation data or \(K\) image-labeled supports with optional masks, and query labels remain unavailable. A unified objective must transfer spatial supervision from masked sources to image-labeled cases, couple weakly supervised localization to high-resolution feature refinement, and avoid interpreting missing masks as normal tissue. The method must produce image-level decisions and detailed anomaly maps without requiring dense target masks. MadCLIP supplies mask-free classification but no documented mixed-source pixel-supervision rule; Spatial-FAD addresses detail loss and UniVAD automates component masks, yet the supplied evidence does not establish their integration into this capability. Evaluation would compare against MVFA and setting-compatible baselines on BMAD’s six datasets, restricting segmentation to BrainMRI, LiverCT and RESC, jointly excluding RESC and OCT17 for retinal zero-shot targets, and using patient-/volume-disjoint partitions with declared support composition and all target normal references counted within \(K\). At matched measured annotation-time budgets with declared source-cost amortization, report image/pixel ROC-AUC, lesion-size-stratified Dice, boundary F1 and lesion sensitivity at fixed false positives per image, using repeated seeds/support draws and paired confidence intervals. Success requires improved small-lesion performance at equal clinician effort, or lower effort at matched performance, rather than fewer labeled images alone.
