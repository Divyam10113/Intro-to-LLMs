## Research gaps and limitations

- **Unseen-domain generalization and external validity remain incompletely established.** Cross-dataset performance does not establish transfer to a modality or anatomy absent from source adaptation. Because RESC and OCT17 both contain retinal OCT, jointly excluding them is necessary for an unseen retinal-OCT evaluation; broader modality- or anatomy-level claims require excluding every dataset sharing the corresponding attribute. Complete source–target folds, model-selection exposure, and permitted target supervision must be disclosed. Dataset holdouts also cannot establish absence of exposure during foundation-model pretraining. Institution-, scanner-, protocol-, and time-disjoint evaluations with locked adaptation procedures remain insufficiently documented; industrial-domain transfer does not substitute for external medical validation.

- **Patient independence and acquisition provenance require stronger documentation.** Correlations among slices, volumes, eyes, and histopathology patches require partitioning at the highest relevant grouping level, generally the patient. The available descriptions do not consistently establish independence among support, validation, calibration, and test groups. LiverCT additionally uses BTCV-derived normal training data and LiTS-derived testing data, while the provenance of labelled support examples requires clarification. These reporting gaps motivate leakage audits, not conclusions that leakage occurred. Patient-grouped partitions, acquisition metadata, and within-source or source-balanced controls are needed to distinguish pathology-sensitive representations from dataset-source confounding.

- **Shot counts do not quantify total annotation efficiency.** “Zero-shot” is target-relative: source adaptation still uses labelled medical data, including lesion masks where available. Few-shot accounting must specify whether \(K\) counts images, examinations, or patients; normal–abnormal composition; mask and organ-metadata requirements; normal-reference verification; and whether reference-bank cases are included. Source-data quantities, mask availability, and class/domain sampling weights also require disclosure. Normal-only comparators operate under a different supervision regime, so existing comparisons cannot independently attribute gains to architecture or annotation efficiency. Comparisons should match supervision, data access, preprocessing, backbone, and tuning resources, or explicitly report cost–performance trade-offs.

- **Support-set robustness and architectural necessity remain unresolved.** Random support selection without repeated draws, initialization variation, and hierarchical uncertainty estimates does not characterize few-shot reliability. Test-patient bootstrapping alone cannot capture support-selection or adaptation variability. Reported improvements are domain-dependent and include regressions. Aggregate ensemble gains over the strongest individual feature level—0.13 percentage points for classification and 0.05 for segmentation—require paired uncertainty estimates and computational comparisons before establishing an accuracy–complexity advantage. The parameter-matched adapter–projector ablation is informative but does not resolve dual-versus-single adapter capacity, independent text/reference-branch contributions, control-model optimization, or sensitivity to training and fusion hyperparameters.

- **The contribution of explicit language semantics is not isolated.** For a fixed organ and two fixed text prototypes, binary similarity-softmax log-odds depend on the visual embedding’s projection onto the difference between those prototypes. Adapter improvements therefore do not, by themselves, demonstrate that medically meaningful prompt semantics drive transfer. Necessary controls include capacity- and supervision-matched image-only heads, geometry- and temperature-matched nonsemantic anchors, and systematic perturbations of templates, synonyms, and organ identities. Clinically enriched prompts should be evaluated for both specificity and held-out-disease sensitivity. Such inference-time controls do not independently identify the contribution of vision–language pretraining.

- **Novel-pathology generalization and anatomy–pathology disentanglement are not directly established.** Dataset, modality, or anatomy holdouts do not isolate disease novelty within a controlled anatomical and acquisition domain. Disease/subtype holdouts must exclude the designated conditions from source supervision, target support, and model selection. Known-anomaly supervision could encourage source-associated shortcuts, but this remains a hypothesis rather than a demonstrated MVFA failure. Separating classification and segmentation adapters does not itself separate normal from abnormal variation or anatomy from pathology. Evaluation should independently vary disease identity, anatomy, and acquisition conditions and include benign variants, postoperative changes, artifacts, and coexisting abnormalities.

- **Token resolution and evaluation granularity constrain localization claims.** At the reported 240-pixel input resolution, features from multiple transformer depths retain approximately the same \(17\times17\) token grid; multidepth extraction is not a high-resolution feature pyramid. Interpolating token scores cannot restore diagnostic information removed by input resizing. Selected MRI slices, windowed CT images, histopathology patches, and supplied organ identities also omit parts of examination-level selection, anatomical identification, and aggregation. Maximum-score pooling is sensitive to extreme responses and examination size and does not explicitly represent lesion burden. Physical-lesion-size-stratified, boundary, full-volume, and whole-slide evaluations are therefore required.

- **Normal-reference matching, score fusion, and uncertainty require systematic validation.** Small normal-reference banks cannot be assumed to represent population variability, while unrestricted nearest-neighbor matching does not guarantee anatomically valid correspondence. Text-softmax scores and cosine distances are not interchangeable probabilistic quantities; equal fusion weights establish neither equal influence nor calibrated disease probabilities. Required analyses include reference-set size and composition sensitivity, constrained versus unconstrained matching, branch-wise score distributions, fusion/calibration ablations, and uncertainty–error and coverage–risk relationships. Verified target-normal examples constitute target supervision and must be declared and budgeted rather than used implicitly to calibrate a nominally source-only model.

- **AUROC alone is insufficient to establish clinical utility.** Ranking performance does not determine actionable sensitivity, false-positive workload, or predictive value at deployment prevalence. Class imbalance alone does not inflate AUROC when class-conditional score distributions are unchanged; nevertheless, high pixel AUROC can conceal missed small lesions, inaccurate boundaries, and poor examination-level detection. Evaluation should prespecify intended use and include lesion sensitivity, false positives per examination, Dice/IoU and boundary measures, precision–recall metrics, examination-level operating points, prevalence-aware predictive values, and subgroup uncertainty. Prospective or reader-assisted validation should assess downstream workload and outcomes within the intended clinical workflow.

- **Preservation of pretrained knowledge and resistance to overfitting remain unverified.** Freezing backbone weights does not preserve downstream representations when internal adapters modify intermediate activations. A small residual coefficient does not control \(\|\gamma A_l(F_l)\|\) without constraints on adapter-output magnitude, and downstream sensitivity can further amplify representational changes. Fewer trainable parameters provide a plausible regularizing bias, not a guarantee of reliable adaptation from very small support sets. Direct pre/post-adaptation retention tests, representation-drift measurements, relative residual norms, residual-weight sensitivity analyses, and repeated-support learning curves are needed to substantiate knowledge-preservation and overfitting-resistance claims.

- **Parameter efficiency has not been established as deployment efficiency.** Reducing trainable parameters does not eliminate the forward cost of a ViT-L/14 backbone or, for end-to-end internal adaptation, gradient propagation through downstream frozen blocks. Multilevel feature storage, task branches, and reference searches add resolution- and bank-size-dependent overheads. Complete examinations and whole slides may substantially alter these costs. Evaluation should report hardware-specified training/adaptation time, latency distributions, peak memory, examination throughput, and accuracy–resource trade-offs. Reference storage, privacy protection, cross-site updates, and drift monitoring also require explicit operational assessment.

**New research problem—Annotation-budgeted, risk-controlled open-set anomaly detection at the examination level.** Given patient-grouped source domains \(\mathcal D_S\) with heterogeneous classification labels and optional lesion masks, learn a prespecified adaptation rule \(f_{\theta,S_K}=\mathcal A_\theta(\mathcal D_S,S_K)\) that maps complete examinations—including volumes and whole slides—to an anomaly probability, lesion localization, and an uncertainty-based acceptance decision. Let \(K\) count distinct target patients contributing adaptation, calibration, or verified-reference annotations, and let \(\Pi_K\) specify support acquisition, screening, and annotation procedures, with \(S_0=\varnothing\). Over an explicitly defined family \(\mathcal Q\) of independently controlled institutional/acquisition, modality–anatomy, and disease shifts, characterize the attainable annotation–risk–computation frontier:
\[
\min_{\theta}\ \sup_{Q\in\mathcal Q}
\mathbb E_{S_K\sim\Pi_K(Q)}
\!\left[
R_{\mathrm{exam}}(f_{\theta,S_K};Q)
+\lambda_{\ell}R_{\mathrm{lesion}}(f_{\theta,S_K};Q)
+\lambda_{p}R_{\mathrm{Brier}}(f_{\theta,S_K};Q)
\right].
\]
Here, \(R_{\mathrm{exam}}\) is an intended-use-specific clinical loss, \(R_{\mathrm{lesion}}\) penalizes missed lesions and localization errors, and \(R_{\mathrm{Brier}}\) is a proper probabilistic scoring risk—not an isolated measure of calibration. All three risks are evaluated on independent test patients, including examinations for which the system abstains. Feasibility requires a total development-annotation budget covering source and target labels, masks, screening, and normal-reference verification; explicit latency and memory limits; upper bounds on automatic false-positive alerts per normal examination and clinical loss conditional on acceptance; and nonzero minimum coverage, including prespecified safety-critical subgroups. Statistical constraints must state their confidence level and sampling assumptions, with infeasible regimes explicitly characterized rather than obscured by aggregate averages. A testable hypothesis is that anatomy-conditioned multiresolution normality modeling improves this frontier; norm-controlled adapters, compressed anatomically valid references, and robust examination aggregation should be evaluated as ablated components rather than presumed necessities. Language conditioning must be compared with capacity-, supervision-, and tuning-matched image-only and nonsemantic-anchor controls. Calibration must use source validation alone for \(K=0\), and only declared, budgeted target annotations for \(K>0\), with statistically valid separation or reuse of fitting and calibration data. No nontrivial calibration or selective-risk guarantee should be claimed for arbitrary unsupported shifts. External validation must lock model-selection, adaptation, and calibration procedures; audit patient independence and acquisition provenance; jointly exclude datasets sharing a claimed held-out modality or anatomy; exclude designated novel diseases from source and support supervision; and quantify support-, seed-, and patient-level uncertainty. Success requires clinically meaningful external improvements in lesion detection, boundary accuracy, examination-level operating points, and selective prediction at matched annotation and computational cost—not merely higher macro-average AUROC.
