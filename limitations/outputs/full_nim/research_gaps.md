## Research gaps and limitations

- **Few-shot estimates and ablations lack statistical support.** All few-shot results derive from a single random K-shot draw, with no repeated runs, error bars or significance tests, and accuracy is non-monotonic in K (HIS AC 85.10 at K=8 versus 82.62 at K=16). The reported ablation margins (0.13 AC for multi-level ensembling, +1.29 AC for the dual adapter) therefore cannot be separated from support-sampling noise, so the abstract's "significantly surpasses" claim is unsupported. [L1, L27 | Experimental Design; Results & Analysis | Inferred; Peer-review; Literature | partially_addressed]
- **Split integrity and leakage are unverified for several benchmarks.** No patient-, volume- or slide-level split verification is reported for BrainMRI, LiverCT, OCT17 or ChestXray, so correlated slices or patches from the same case may straddle the train/test boundary; even where slide separation holds (HIS), patch-level AUCs from 115 WSIs are treated as independent observations. BMAD-based work documents slide/patient separation for HIS and RESC only, and no later study re-runs MVFA under leakage-free splits. [L2 | Experimental Design | Inferred; Peer-review; Literature | partially_addressed]
- **"Zero-shot" transfer still depends on dense medical supervision.** Adapters are trained on labeled images and pixel masks from five source datasets with unreported per-fold budgets, and the resulting supervised model is compared against the training-free WinCLIP. Later "strictly zero-shot" work (TAFT) and target-free frameworks still train on source domains and report no label/mask accounting, so the gains cannot be read as transfer without medical labels. [L3 | Experimental Design | Inferred; Peer-review | partially_addressed]

- **The multi-level alignment loss is undefined without pixel masks.** Dice and Focal terms are applied at every level to all datasets, yet OCT17, ChestXray and HIS provide only image-level labels and the treatment of missing masks is never specified, so the effective objective differs across folds and is non-reproducible on half the benchmark. ## Research problem

CLIP-based few-shot medical anomaly detectors assume supervision sites rarely provide: MadCLIP, TAFT and MVFA's text-adapted dual-path follow-up train on expert pixel masks, score test images against a normal-only memory bank, and fuse uncalibrated softmax and cosine-distance scores. The problem is to build a mask-free, support-composition-robust detector that uses normal and abnormal labeled support images symmetrically as test-time references and returns one calibrated score. The setting is multi-modality medical anomaly detection (MRI, CT, OCT, X-ray, histology) with a frozen CLIP backbone and MVFA-style multi-level residual adapters. At training time it assumes source-domain labeled images (masks optional) plus K target-domain image-level labels; at test time only unlabeled target images and the K support images, with no target masks or per-target retraining. It must (i) replace mask-dependent Dice/Focal alignment with an objective well defined under image-level supervision alone, (ii) learn a bidirectional reference model in which abnormal exemplars constrain the boundary at test time, (iii) remain stable across balanced and unbalanced support compositions, and (iv) fuse text and exemplar evidence by calibrated rather than fixed-weight combination. None provides these jointly: MadCLIP adds a learned abnormality branch but still requires dense masks and keeps abnormal exemplars out of the reference set; UniVAD is training-free and normal-only; TAFT retains region masks and single-draw K=4 evaluation. Success will be measured on the six BMAD benchmarks with patient/slide-level splits and repeated K ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16} draws (balanced and unbalanced), reporting image-level AUROC/AUPRC, ECE/Brier calibration, sensitivity at fixed specificity, pixel-level PRO and Dice, per-anomaly-type results for OCT17/ChestXray, and matched-budget comparisons against WinCLIP, APRIL-GAN, AnomalyCLIP, MadCLIP, UniVAD and TAFT.
